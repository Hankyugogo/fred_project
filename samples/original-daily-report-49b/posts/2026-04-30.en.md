Overnight Lead
The main overnight story was not one headline, but a collision between rates and earnings. When inflation interpretation turned more hawkish, tech was pressured; when earnings relief appeared, part of the loss was retraced, creating frequent intraday style shifts. Geopolitical risk remained a background driver, but without broader confirmed enforcement, markets mostly absorbed it through sector rotation rather than full index de-risking. The reversal risk today is simple: if yields break higher again, that fragile balance can unwind quickly.

Short commentary
Right now, what matters most is not just what happened, but whether it moved yields in hard numbers. Markets are pricing rate direction faster than they price narrative. The same headline can trigger relief if yields fall, or profit-taking if yields rise.

For geopolitics, separate commentary from execution. Talk-only phases can lift oil briefly and fade, while confirmed sanctions or transport disruption can change volatility regime. Track the reaction path in order: event, then oil/vol, then rates, then index and sectors.

For Korean investors today, treat US yields and the dollar as a combined risk switch. If both rise together, valuation-sensitive areas like growth and battery names can weaken first. If yields stabilize and dollar momentum cools, oversold tech may get a tactical bounce window.
